RandomStorytellingThe world

In Austin, everybody is working on themselves.

You cold plunge before work. You breathe in a box. You keep a gratitude journal you have not opened since March. There is a sound bath on your block and a man at the farmers market selling mushrooms for focus. You have read every book about feeling better, and you still feel like this.

And somewhere in a high-rise on Rainey Street, one man has decided to take all of it literally.

22 Levels02 / 14
RandomStorytellingPremise

A man in Austin tries to feel better one notch at a time, and every attempt to climb goes a little too far.

This is a comedy about two neighbors in one Austin high-rise, at the center of which is Lawrence, a man who finds a 22-step emotional scale and decides to climb it one step per episode. The story is about his climb, the neighbor who keeps grading it honestly, and the gap between every self-help idea and real life.

22 Levels03 / 14
RandomStorytellingThe engine

One step up. Every episode.

The scale runs from despair at the bottom to joy at the top, and the rule is you can only move up one step at a time. So every episode starts Lawrence on one step and ends him exactly one step higher.

The format gives the show its season count: 22 steps, 22 episodes. When he reaches joy, he slips, and the climb starts over.

RandomStorytellingCharacters
Played by Lawrence Whitaker III

Lawrence

Means well. Reads every self-help idea as an instruction and carries it out too hard. He never waits for life to happen to him: he books the room, prints the sign, starts the argument. He is new to the ladder, so the audience learns the rules with him.

His tote bag: six self-help books with color-coded tabs, a laminator, a timer, and a gratitude journal with one entry.

His secret: he has read every book on feeling better and has never once said out loud that he feels bad.

Played by Natalie Howard

Natalie

The honest grader. Where Lawrence overshoots, she sees the actual step he is on and says so. She has her own ladder, and some weeks her climb drives the story while he gets in the way.

Her binder: every building rule, tabbed and annotated, plus several she added herself.

Her secret: she wrote half of those rules. [Natalie's input welcome here.]

RandomStorytellingPilot
Level 10 · Frustration

Int. Sky lounge, 16th floor · Austin · Day

Why todayDay one of Lawrence's ladder. He has found the scale, decided he lives on step 10, and booked the sky lounge for one hour to start climbing. The hour is the clock.
OpeningLawrence alone in the elevator, rehearsing his gratitude out loud to his reflection. The doors open. Natalie is already at the window, with a binder.
Act oneThe window is reserved. Not the lounge. The window.
Act twoHe escalates on purpose: a polite argument over the building rules, clause by clause. Nobody raises their voice. Both invent new rules as they go.
Act threeWorst case. Gratitude, forgiveness, generosity, and each one backfires. He gives her the whole lounge, then needs it back. A sign on the glass reads "The view is currently occupied," above a brass plaque with her name.
Act fourThe clock. Ten minutes left, and the yoga resident is at the door with a mat. The skyline does the talking.
Act fiveHe lands one step up: people are like this, and that is fine. Pessimism, played as total relief.
TagHe books the rooftop grill for a slot years from now and tapes up a laminated sign. Natalie reads it, nods, and adds it to her binder. Who actually wrote the rules?
