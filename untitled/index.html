RandomStorytellingThe engine

One step up. Every episode.

There is a well-known 22-step emotional scale. Despair sits at the bottom, joy at the top, and you can only ever move up one step at a time.

Lawrence believes this completely. Every episode starts him on one step and ends him exactly one step higher. He reaches for gratitude and lands in a feud. He tries forgiveness and ends up holding a laminated sign. Against all evidence, he climbs.

RandomStorytellingCharacters
Played by Lawrence Whitaker III

Lawrence

Means well. Reads every self-help idea as an instruction and carries it out too hard. Shy until he isn't. He never waits for life to happen to him: he books the room, writes the sign, starts the argument. Every better-feeling thought overshoots, and he lands one step up looking like he won something.

What he hides: he has read every book on feeling better and has never once admitted he feels bad.

Played by Natalie Howard

Natalie

The honest grader. Where Lawrence overshoots, Natalie sees the actual step he is on and says so. She has her own ladder, and some episodes her climb drives the story while Lawrence gets in the way. In the pilot, she is the neighbor who believes the building rules are written in stone.

What she hides: she wrote half of those rules herself. [Natalie's input welcome here.]

RandomStorytellingPilot
Level 10 · Frustration

Int. Sky lounge, 16th floor · Austin · Day

Why todayDay one of Lawrence's ladder. He has finally found the 22-step scale, decided he lives on step 10, and booked the sky lounge for one hour to start climbing. The hour is the clock.
Act oneHe arrives with a plan. Natalie is already at the window with a binder. The window is reserved. Not the lounge. The window.
Act twoHe escalates on purpose: a polite argument over the building rules, clause by clause. Nobody raises their voice. Both invent new rules as they go.
Act threeWorst case. He tries gratitude, forgiveness, generosity, and each one backfires. He gives her the whole lounge, then needs it back. A sign on the glass reads "The view is currently occupied," above a brass plaque with her name.
Act fourThe clock. Ten minutes left on his booking, and the next resident is at the door with a yoga mat. The skyline does the talking.
Act fiveHe lands somewhere new: people are like this, and that is fine. Pessimism, one full step up, played as total relief.
TagHe books the rooftop grill for a slot years from now and tapes up a laminated sign. Natalie reads it, nods, and adds it to her binder.

RandomStorytellingWhy Austin, how we make it

Why Austin

A city of people working on themselves: cold plunges, breathwork, gratitude journals, a wellness studio on every corner. The perfect place for a man who takes all of it literally, with a deep bench of local actors and filmmakers ready to make something that looks like home.

How we make it

Each episode is built from a one-page outline and improvised on set. One shoot day makes one episode plus three vertical cuts. One shoot day every two weeks with a consistent Austin crew.

Austin brands, inside the story.

A brand never sits on a wall. Its product is what knocks Lawrence down a step, or what lifts him up one.

BackgroundPresent in the world of the scene
