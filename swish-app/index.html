What you are buying

A trainer gets an hour with a player, then loses sight of them. The player shoots alone. No eye on the form. No way to tell which miss is worth fixing.

Swish is that eye, on the player’s own phone. It films a shot and reads the form from body pose. It scores nine mechanics against ideal ranges. Then it names the one fix with the most leverage, not a list of everything wrong.

A trainer seat is the player app plus a roster. It films the trainer’s own reps. It adds a seven day log for every player: shots, days, average, change versus last week, best, and the fix showing up most.

The nine mechanics: leg load, elbow set, elbow flare, set point height, release arc, follow through, balance, stance width and drift. The feedback comes in one of five coaching voices: The Shot Doctor, The Skills Trainer, The Hype, The Professor and The Fundamentalist. They are archetypes and composites. No real coach, athlete, brand or trademarked system is named anywhere in the app. That is deliberate.

How do I fix my jump shot?

Fix one thing at a time. A list of everything wrong is hard to act on. Swish films a shot, scores nine mechanics against ideal ranges and names the one fix with the most leverage.

How do I improve my shooting form in basketball?

Film your shot, find the one mechanic that is furthest off, work on that, then film again. Swish does the reading for you: leg load, elbow set, elbow flare, set point height, release arc, follow through, balance, stance width and drift.

Where should my elbow be when shooting a basketball?

Most coaches teach the elbow under the ball and in line with the rim, not flared out to the side. Swish measures both elbow set and elbow flare on every shot you film.

Why is my basketball shot flat?

A flat shot usually means a low release arc. Swish measures release arc on every shot and tells you when that is the fix with the most leverage.
